## 1. Introduction

1. LF elimination depends on consecutive effective MDA rounds; missed rounds delay it.
2. Programmes must decide before the year where to put scarce supervision.
3. Fuller et al. explained coverage of delivered rounds; nobody has tested whether
   non-delivery can be predicted from public data, or whether a model beats a rule.
4. ESPEN data have known quality problems (Ng et al.), so the outcome itself needs audit.
5. Aim and three objectives (below).

## 2. Objectives

1. Construct and audit a multi-country IU-year panel that separates reported
   interruptions from delivered rounds, non-reporting, never-started programmes and
   legitimate pauses.
2. Compare a fixed history rule, penalised logistic regression and CatBoost, with and
   without context predictors, under a programme-information and a public-data scenario.
3. Quantify performance on later years and on countries left out of training,
   including calibration and the share of interruptions captured at 10% and 20% capacity.

Pre-specified nulls: history alone may be enough; context may add nothing; country may
dominate.

## 5. Discussion

- Principal findings, stated with their uncertainty.
- Why country-level shocks limit district-level prediction, and what that means for
  where supervision decisions should be informed (country and partner funding
  information versus district history).
- Comparison with Fuller et al.: different outcome, complementary conclusion.
- Strengths: public data, scripted acquisition, pre-specified protocol, locked test,
  leakage tests, country-clustered uncertainty.
- Limitations: `docs/ISSUES.md`, in that order.
- Implications: what a programme could and could not do with a history rule; the case
  for publishing planning and funding status alongside treatment data.
- Next steps: expert label review, JRSM data, prospective evaluation.
